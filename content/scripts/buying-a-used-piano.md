**Pillar:** Buying & ownership · **Series:** Buyer's Notebook · **Launch slot:** Week 1, post 3

## Concept & format

A quick, practical buyer's guide that positions you as the person to trust on used pianos, without selling anything.

| Item | Plan |

| Length | About 60–70 seconds (~165 words) |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical; keep the host centered so a 16:9 crop works |

## Refined script

Your structure and claims are unchanged. The edits shorten sentences for speaking, add a stronger hook, and give each point one thing viewers can actually do.

**0:00–0:05 — Hook**

ON SCREEN: 3 THINGS TO CHECK BEFORE BUYING A USED PIANO

VO: "That free piano on Marketplace? It could be a great find — or a really expensive project. Here are three things to check first."

**0:05–0:22 — 1. Age**

ON SCREEN: 1. HOW OLD IS IT?

VO: "First: how old is it? Pianos don't last forever, and climate matters. Here in Michigan, once a piano hits about 50 or 60 years old, we often see ones that need substantial work. Older doesn't automatically mean bad — but age should shape your decision. Tip: look for the serial number inside. It can tell you the year it was built."

**0:22–0:40 — 2. Maintenance**

ON SCREEN: 2. WHEN WAS IT LAST TUNED — AND REGULATED?

VO: "Second: ask when it was last tuned, and when it was last regulated. Tuning should happen at least once a year. Regulation is different — it's adjusting the thousands of moving parts inside so every key plays evenly. We recommend that about every seven to ten years, depending on use. If the seller doesn't know, that tells you something too."

**0:40–0:54 — 3. Inspection**

ON SCREEN: 3. GET A PRO TO LOOK AT IT

VO: "And third: have a professional technician inspect it. The biggest problems usually aren't visible from the outside. A small inspection fee can tell you exactly what you're buying."

**0:54–1:05 — Recap & close**

ON SCREEN: AGE · MAINTENANCE · INSPECTION

VO: "So: check the age, ask about maintenance, and get a pro's opinion. It's the difference between finding a great piano — and buying an expensive project."

**Notes on the edits**

- **Hook:** "That free piano on Marketplace" is specific and familiar, so it stops the scroll faster than a general question. Your original opening line works as a backup.
- **Serial-number tip:** gives point one something viewers can do today. Cut it if you'd rather keep the video shorter.
- **"If the seller doesn't know…":** a quick red flag that makes point two feel practical, not just informative.
- **Engagement option:** end with "What's the oldest piano you've played?" if you want a comment prompt like the brush video.

## Production notes

- **Show, don't just tell.** Every point has a hands-on visual (serial number, tuning hammer, action, flashlight), which keeps viewers watching between talking-head moments.
- **Use a real older piano.** Visible wear on shot 4 makes the age point land without any extra words.
- **Keep the listing generic.** Make your own mock listing for shot 1 so no real seller appears on screen.
- **Caption everything,** and keep the numbered titles on screen for the whole section so muted viewers can follow.
- **End on playing.** Closing with a well-maintained piano sounding great shows the payoff of the advice.
