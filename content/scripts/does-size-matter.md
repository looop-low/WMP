# Does Size Matter? — Script & Storyboard

Oct 19, 2026 · Seth

**Pillar:** How pianos work · **Series:** Under the Lid · **Launch slot:** Not yet scheduled (could swap in for Week 4, post 11)

## Concept & format

A playful title with a real lesson: walk down a row of grands from the same series, smallest to largest, and let viewers hear what each extra foot buys. The demo carries the video, and the talking supports it.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Length | About 75 seconds (~170 words of VO plus two playing demos) |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical; the row of grands also works as a 16:9 wide shot |
| Talent | You on camera, talking and playing |
| Setting | Your showroom row: same maker, same series, in size order |
| Goal | Teach something people can hear for themselves; get them commenting on what they noticed |
| Tone | Curious and fair: bigger has real advantages, but it isn't right for everyone |

## Refined script

Your explanation and demo moments are kept. The edits tighten the wording and remove a repeated paragraph.

**0:00–0:05 — Hook**

ON SCREEN: DOES SIZE MATTER?

VO: "Does the size of a piano actually matter? Absolutely, and probably more than you think."

**0:05–0:15 — The row**

VO: "In our showroom, I keep pianos from the same maker and the same series side by side, in order of size. It's one of the best ways to hear what you get when you move up."

**0:15–0:24 — The bass**

ON SCREEN: LONGER BASS STRINGS

VO: "As a grand gets bigger, one of the biggest changes is string length, especially in the bass. Longer bass strings produce those low frequencies with less compromise. And you can hear it."

**0:24–0:34 — Demo 1: same low passage, small vs. large**

[Play the same low passage on the smaller, then the larger piano.]

**0:34–0:42 — What you heard**

VO: "The bass gets cleaner and more defined. More of the actual pitch, less of that thuddy, congested sound a shorter piano can have."

**0:42–0:50 — Beyond the bass**

ON SCREEN: BIGGER SOUNDBOARD · LONGER SCALE · MORE RANGE

VO: "But it's not just the bass. Moving up usually means a larger soundboard and a longer scale: more resonance, more power, and a wider dynamic range."

**0:50–1:00 — Demo 2: same passage across the row**

[Play the same short passage on each size, soft to loud.]

VO (over the end of the demo): "A bigger piano isn't just louder. It gives you a bigger palette, from a delicate pianissimo to a full fortissimo."

**1:00–1:15 — Close**

ON SCREEN: WHICH ONE SOUNDED BEST TO YOU?

VO: "That doesn't mean bigger is better for everyone. Your room, your budget and how you'll use it all matter, and there are some phenomenal smaller pianos. But if you've ever wondered why you'd pay more for another foot of piano, play them side by side. The difference might surprise you. Which one sounded best to you?"

**Notes on the edits**

- **Removed the repeated paragraph.** "Why would I pay more just to get another foot of piano?" appeared twice; it now appears once, in the close.
- **The close ends on a question, not an invitation,** to match the strategy's "teach, don't pitch" rule. Your West Michigan Piano invite works well as this video's caption, or as the close if you count this as one of your occasional product posts.
- **The "pianissimo to fortissimo" line now plays over demo 2,** so viewers hear the palette while you describe it.

## Production notes

- **Audio is the whole point.** Use the same mic, in the same spot relative to each piano, at the same gain for every demo, and don't add music under the playing. If levels differ, viewers will hear the recording instead of the piano.
- **Same player, same passage, same touch.** Pick a short low passage (4–6 seconds) that clearly shows bass clarity, and a phrase with a quiet start and a loud finish for demo 2.
- **Note the sizes on screen.** Labeling each piano's length in shot 9 lets viewers connect what they hear to the extra foot.
- **Tune first.** Have every piano in the row freshly tuned before filming so tuning differences don't get mistaken for size differences.
- **Remind people about phone speakers.** A caption like "Best with headphones" helps the bass difference come through.
- **Caption everything;** most viewers watch muted, and the size labels carry the demos even without sound.
