# Paint With Every Brush — Video Script & Storyboard

Oct 19, 2026 · Seth

**Pillar:** How pianos work · **Series:** Under the Lid · **Launch slot:** Week 1, post 1

## Concept & format

One artist paints the same flower twice: once with a single medium brush (the digital piano), once with a full brush set (the acoustic piano). The side-by-side reveal makes the case for acoustic without putting digital down.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 60 seconds (a 30-second cut is noted in the script) |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical for Reels, Shorts and TikTok; frame the canvases so a 16:9 crop also works |
| Talent | One painter, hands and canvas on camera; face optional |
| Voice | Voiceover, warm and conversational, ~140 words |
| Goal | Teach one memorable idea, spark comments, and bring viewers to the page |
| Tone | Informative, not salesy: the full set is worth learning from day one |

## Script

Voiceover runs about 140 words at a relaxed pace. On-screen text is in caps; everything else is spoken.

**0:00–0:04 — Hook**

ON SCREEN: DIGITAL PIANO (left canvas) · ACOUSTIC PIANO (right canvas)

VO: "This is a digital piano. And this is an acoustic piano. Same artist, same flower. Watch what changes."

**0:04–0:08 — The one brush**

VO: "Learning on a digital piano is like painting with one good, medium brush."

**0:08–0:22 — Speed lapse, left canvas**

VO: "And you can make something beautiful with it. The shape is there. The color is there. But every stroke comes out about the same width, the same edge, the same texture. A digital piano gives you the notes. What it can give back from your touch has a ceiling."

**0:22–0:26 — The turn**

ON SCREEN: NOW, THE WHOLE SET.

VO: "An acoustic piano hands you the whole set."

**0:26–0:44 — Speed lapse, right canvas**

VO: "A whisper-soft pianissimo — that's your finest detail brush. A full fortissimo — a wide, flat brush. Half-pedaling, the soft pedal changing the color, strings ringing in sympathy with each other — those are the brushes for the details nobody points to, but everyone feels."

**0:44–0:48 — Reveal**

ON SCREEN: SAME FLOWER. SAME ARTIST. DIFFERENT TOOLS.

VO: "Same flower. Same artist. Different tools."

**0:48–1:00 — Close**

ON SCREEN: WHICH ONE DID YOU LEARN ON?

VO: "You can start with that one brush and adapt to the full set later. But learn with the whole set from the start, and it becomes part of how you think about music. So which one did you learn on? Tell me in the comments."

**30-second cut:** keep the hook, cut 0:08–0:22 to one line ("The flower's there — but every stroke looks the same"), trim the right-canvas VO to the pianissimo/fortissimo pair, then reveal and close.

## Production notes

- **Match the flowers.** Lightly pencil the same outline on both canvases first, so the only visible difference is the brushwork.
- **Lock the camera.** Use one fixed position for both speed lapses, same lighting and exposure, so the comparison is fair. Shoot each painting in real time (roughly 20–30 minutes) and speed it up in the edit.
- **Let the music make the point.** Score the left half with a digital piano and the right half with an acoustic, same piece, same player. Viewers will hear the difference before the VO explains it.
- **Pick a flower with range.** A peony, rose or iris has soft petals, sharp edges and fine stamens, which gives the full brush set something to show off.
- **Caption everything.** Burn in captions; most viewers watch muted.
- **End on a question, not a pitch.** "Which one did you learn on?" invites easy comments from digital and acoustic players alike, and replying to those comments keeps people coming back to the page.
