# Is It Still a Steinway? — Script & Storyboard

Oct 19, 2026 · Seth

**Pillar:** Buying & ownership · **Series:** Buyer's Notebook · **Launch slot:** Not yet scheduled

## Concept & format

A piano version of the Ship of Theseus: if you replace nearly every part, is it still the same instrument? The thought experiment makes it entertaining, and the takeaway is practical: "rebuilt" is a starting point for questions, not an answer.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Length | About 90 seconds (~230 words). Your full draft runs about 3 minutes and works as a YouTube long-form version |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical; keep the host and the open piano centered |
| Talent | You on camera; a technician or rebuilder for workshop shots if possible |
| Setting | Workshop or showroom with a grand opened up, ideally a rim with the action out, plus loose parts: hammers, action, pinblock section |
| Goal | Help buyers ask better questions; build trust as the people who explain even when it isn't your piano |
| Tone | Fair and curious. Respect independent rebuilders while being clear about what changes |

**One to watch:** since you sell Steinways, this topic could look self-serving. The script's fairness lines ("phenomenal independent rebuilders," "even if the piano isn't ours") are what keep it credible, so keep them in.

## Refined script

Your argument, the Steinway example and the question list are kept. The edits shorten it for short-form and bring the "is it still a Steinway?" question to the front.

**0:00–0:06 — Hook**

ON SCREEN: IS IT STILL A STEINWAY?

VO: "Is a Steinway still a Steinway if the only original thing left is the rim? If you're shopping for a rebuilt piano, you need to think about this."

**0:06–0:20 — "Rebuilt" means a lot of things**

ON SCREEN: "REBUILT" = ?

VO: "Because 'rebuilt' can mean almost anything. One rebuilder replaces the action. Another restrings it but keeps the original pinblock. Someone else replaces the soundboard, pinblock, strings, hammers and action, nearly everything inside. And a properly rebuilt piano can be phenomenal."

**0:20–0:42 — Why parts matter**

ON SCREEN: SOUNDBOARD · PINBLOCK · HAMMERS · ACTION · SCALE

VO: "But what parts went into it? Take Steinway. They don't build a case and fill it with generic parts. Over generations they've developed specific designs meant to work together: the diaphragmatic soundboard, the Octagrip pinblock, their hammers, the action geometry, the scaling. That's what makes the Steinway sound and touch, and they still make those parts to their own specifications."

**0:42–0:56 — A design decision**

VO: "So when you replace those with parts made by somebody else, that's a design decision. It's not automatically a bad one. There are extremely talented independent rebuilders out there. But it probably won't be the piano Steinway designed."

**0:56–1:12 — The questions to ask**

ON SCREEN: WHO REBUILT IT? · WHAT WAS REPLACED? · WHAT WAS KEPT? · WHOSE PARTS? · WHEN?

VO: "So if someone says 'rebuilt Steinway,' don't stop at 'rebuilt.' Ask who rebuilt it, what they replaced, what they kept, whose parts they used, and when. Two 'rebuilt Steinways' can be completely different pianos."

**1:12–1:30 — The big question & close**

ON SCREEN: A STEINWAY, OR A REBUILDER'S INTERPRETATION?

VO: "Replace almost everything that makes it sound and play the way it does, and at some point you have to ask: are you buying a Steinway, or a rebuilder's interpretation of one? There's no simple answer. But if you're spending that kind of money, you deserve to know exactly what's inside. And if you're not sure what you're looking at, give us a call. We're happy to help, even if the piano isn't ours."

**Notes on the edits**

- **Spelling check:** Steinway calls it the **Octagrip** pinblock (your draft says "octogrip"). Worth confirming the on-screen spelling.
- **"Pear-shaped hammers" was simplified to "their hammers."** Put it back if you can point to Steinway's own wording, since this video's credibility depends on getting details exactly right.
- **The patent line was folded into "make those parts to their own specifications"** to save time; the long version can keep it.
- **Kept your close.** "Even if the piano isn't ours" reads as helpful, not salesy, so it fits the caring pillar. If you'd rather end on a question: "Where would you draw the line?"

## Production notes

- **Open on the empty rim.** It's the most unusual image in the video, and it makes the hook question visual before you say a word.
- **Get the facts exactly right.** Check component names and claims against Steinway's own materials before filming. A knowledgeable comment section will notice any slip.
- **Show rebuilders respectfully.** If you show third-party parts or another shop's work, keep it generic and unbranded, and never present a specific rebuilder's piano as the cautionary example.
- **Make the question list screenshot-friendly.** Shot 8's checklist is what people will save, so keep it on screen long enough to read.
- **Caption everything;** keep part names on screen as they're mentioned.
- **Long version:** your full draft runs as-is on YouTube with longer workshop footage. It's a natural place to include the patent history.
