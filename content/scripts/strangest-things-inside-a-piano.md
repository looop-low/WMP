# Strangest Things Found Inside a Piano — Plan & Storyboard

Oct 19, 2026 · Seth

**Pillar:** Fun & challenges · **Series:** Shop Talk (new) · **Launch slot:** Not yet scheduled

## Concept & format

A fast montage of real answers: you go around to technicians and coworkers, ask one question, and cut together the best stories, building to the weirdest. Your team does the entertaining, and viewers meet the people behind the business.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 60–75 seconds; 5–7 answers of 6–10 seconds each |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical, each person framed the same way |
| Talent | You asking the questions off camera; techs and employees answering |
| Setting | Wherever each person works: workshop bench, showroom floor, inside an open piano |
| Goal | Entertain, show the team's personality, and get viewers sharing their own stories |
| Tone | Light, surprised, a little funny, with one useful tip in the middle |

**Why it works:** it touches all three reputation goals. It's entertaining, it shows the expertise of the people who work inside pianos every day, and the tip shows you care about people's instruments.

## Interview questions

**The main question (ask everyone the same way):**

> "What's the strangest thing you've ever found inside a piano?"

**Follow-ups (pick one or two depending on the story):**

- Where in the piano was it?
- How do you think it got in there?
- What did the owner say when you showed them?
- Did it affect how the piano played or sounded?
- Do you still have it?

**Bonus questions (for spare clips and future shorts):**

- What's the most common thing you find?
- What's the best thing you've ever found? Money, a letter, something valuable?
- What do you wish people would stop putting on or near their pianos?

**Tips for usable answers**

- **Ask them to start with the question in their answer:** "The strangest thing I ever found in a piano was…" That way each clip makes sense on its own, without your voice.
- **Ask them to show the object** if they kept it, or point to where in the piano it was.
- **Let them tell the full story,** then ask for the short version: "Now tell me that in one sentence." Use whichever works better in the edit.
- **Get a reaction shot:** after the answer, ask "How did you react?" A laugh or a face is gold for the cut.

## Script skeleton

The interviews fill most of the runtime. This is the frame you edit them into, with your lines in VO or on camera.

**0:00–0:04 — Cold open**

Your second-best answer, mid-sentence, with no intro. Save the best one for the end.

**0:04–0:08 — Title**

ON SCREEN: STRANGEST THINGS FOUND INSIDE A PIANO

YOU (to camera or VO): "I asked our techs and team one question: what's the strangest thing you've ever found inside a piano?"

**0:08–0:40 — The montage**

4–5 answers, each 6–8 seconds, ordered from funny to strange. Put each person's first name and role on screen, e.g. MIKE · PIANO TECHNICIAN.

**0:40–0:50 — The useful bit**

ON SCREEN: QUICK TIP

YOU: "Here's the thing: most of this falls in through the gap above the keys or under the lid. If a key starts sticking or you hear a new buzz or rattle, don't go fishing for it yourself. Have a technician take a look. It's usually a quick fix."

**0:50–1:02 — The winner**

The strangest answer, played in full with the object reveal and the reaction.

**1:02–1:10 — Close**

ON SCREEN: WHAT'S THE STRANGEST THING YOU'VE FOUND IN A PIANO?

YOU: "Okay, your turn. What's the strangest thing you've ever found in a piano? Tell me in the comments."

## Production notes

- **Get permission.** Ask each person whether they're comfortable appearing on camera, and use first names only unless they prefer otherwise.
- **Protect customers.** Never name the customer or show anything personal that was found: letters, photos, documents with names or addresses. Describe those instead of showing them.
- **Interview more people than you need.** Talk to 8–10 people to get 5–7 great answers. Spare answers become standalone 15-second shorts later.
- **Use a lav mic.** Workshops are noisy; a clip-on mic keeps every answer clear.
- **Keep the framing consistent.** The same shot size and height for every person makes the montage feel deliberate.
- **Collect the objects.** If people kept their finds, gather them for the "collection" shot. It makes a great thumbnail.
- **Caption everything,** including names and roles.
