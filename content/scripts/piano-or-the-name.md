# Are You Buying the Piano—or the Name? — Script & Storyboard

Oct 19, 2026 · Seth

**Pillar:** Buying & ownership · **Series:** Buyer's Notebook · **Launch slot:** Week 2, post 4

## Concept & format

An honest myth-buster: the name on the fallboard tells you what a piano was built to be, not what it is today. It cuts both ways: don't overtrust a famous name, and don't write one off over a single bad piano.

| Item | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Length | About 80 seconds (~220 words). Your full draft runs about 3.5 minutes and works as a YouTube long-form version |
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical; keep the host centered so a 16:9 crop works |
| Talent | You on camera as host |
| Setting | Showroom with a few new pianos of one model, one well-kept grand, and one tired older piano |
| Goal | Build trust as the honest expert; earn shares and comments |
| Tone | Balanced and candid: "brand matters, but it isn't the whole story" |

## Refined script

Your argument and structure are kept: what a name tells you, the trap, the flip side, and the takeaway. The edits cut the draft to about a third of its length for short-form, mostly by merging repeated points.

**0:00–0:06 — Hook**

ON SCREEN: ARE YOU BUYING THE PIANO — OR THE NAME?

VO: "Don't buy a piano just because of the name on the fallboard. Here's why."

**0:06–0:22 — What a name tells you**

VO: "Brand and model tell you a lot about what a piano was like when it was new. Major manufacturers are remarkably consistent. And some names earned their reputation. Steinway has spent more than 170 years refining its pianos, and there's a reason you'll find them on concert stages all over the world."

**0:22–0:42 — The trap**

ON SCREEN: BUILT TO BE ≠ CONDITION TODAY

VO: "But a piano is mostly wood, felt and leather, and all of that changes with age, use, climate and care. So the name tells you what the piano was built to be, not what condition it's in today. How old is it? Does it hold a tuning? What shape are the hammers, strings, soundboard and action in? Has it been regulated and voiced?"

**0:42–1:05 — The flip side**

ON SCREEN: WAS IT THE BRAND — OR THE CONDITION?

VO: "It works the other way, too. Maybe you played one piano from a great brand, didn't like it, and wrote off the whole name. But was that piano 40 or 50 years old? Was it ever regulated or voiced? Before you decide, play a new one, or a few well-kept used ones. Sometimes what you disliked wasn't the brand. It was that piano's condition."

**1:05–1:20 — Close**

ON SCREEN: BUY THE PIANO, NOT THE NAME.

VO: "Brand absolutely matters. Every maker has its own sound, feel and design philosophy. Just don't confuse the name you recognize most with the piano you'll love most. Use the name to guide your search, then buy the piano in front of you."

**Notes on the edits**

- **Cut "reputation vs. recognition":** it's a sharp idea, but it needs explaining. "Built to be vs. condition today" makes the same point faster.
- **Merged the two "brand matters" passages** into the close so the video doesn't hedge twice.
- **The checklist questions stay in full;** they double as on-screen text in shots 6–7.
- **Comment prompt option:** end with "Has a piano ever surprised you, good or bad?"

## Production notes

- **Decide what the tired piano shows.** Covering or angling away from its fallboard keeps the point about condition and avoids reading as a knock on any brand. Showing a worn piano from a respected maker makes the point more strongly, but some viewers may take it personally.
- **A/B the same chord.** Shots 8 and 10 should use the same chord, framing and mic position so the difference is obviously the piano, not the edit.
- **Keep the Steinway moment short.** One line and one shot reads as context, not an ad.
- **Checklist as text.** The question list in shot 7 is what people pause and screenshot, so keep it legible and on screen long enough to read.
- **Caption everything;** most viewers watch muted.
- **Long version:** your full draft can run as-is on YouTube, using this storyboard with longer holds and more A/B playing.
